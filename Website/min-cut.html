Cuts

Given an undirected graph G with n vertices and m edges, a cut refers to the partition of the vertices of G into two disjoint and nonempty subsets by removing a subset of edges from G.

The value of a cut is equal to the number of edges removed, or in a weighted graph, the sum of edge weights that are removed to partition the graph. The minimum cut problem wishes to minimise the value of the cut.

Formally worded, a cut of an undirected graph G = (V, E) with n = |V| vertices and m = |E| edges, is a partition of the vertices of G into two nonempty sets A and B. An edge (v, w) crosses the cut of A and B if one of v and w is in A and the other in B. The value of a cut is the number of edges that cross the cut or, in a weighted graph, the sum of the weights of the edges that cross the cut. The minimum cut problem is to find a cut of minimum value.

It is assumed that the graph G is connected, otherwise the solution is trivial. We also require that all edge weights be nonnegative because otherwise the problem is NP-complete by a trivial transformation from the maximum-cut problem.

Global vs. s-t Minimum Cuts

A closely related problem is the s–t minimum cut, which asks for a minimum value cut with a given source s on one side, and a sink t on the other. By the Max-flow Min-cut Theorem, its value equals the maximum s–t flow, which follows that it can be computed by any max-flow algorithm. This project instead seeks to address the global minimum cut, the minimum over all pairs s, t of the minimum s–t cut. Fixing s and trying every t would need n − 1 max-flow computations. To avoid this, we will be discussing the Stoer-Wagner Algorithm.
